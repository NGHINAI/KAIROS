# 01 — IDLE: The Thesis That Survived the Teardown

> The strategic core, forged in an adversarial investor grilling. If `00-MASTER-PLAN.md` is *how we go to market*, this is *what we're actually building and why it could be big*. Read after the master plan.
> **Locked 2026-06-08.**

---

## 1. The category thesis (not a feature — a thesis)

**Software is the wrong abstraction. Outcomes are the abstraction.**

For 40 years you've *operated* software — you open the app, you drive, the tool assists. IDLE's bet is that this ends: you state outcomes and approve decisions, and execution happens beneath you across every app. The behavior IDLE creates is **the end of operating software** — the way Search created "look it up before asking," and Slack created "message instead of email."

This is venture-scale because it's a platform-shift claim, not an "AI assistant for X." But platform shifts are a graveyard of people who predicted the future correctly and died anyway. The only question that matters is not *"will software become agentic?"* (yes) but **"why does IDLE capture the value?"** The rest of this doc is the honest answer.

---

## 2. The user — narrower and more honest than "chief of staff"

Do not assume users want to be **outcome supervisors**. Most don't. Many derive confidence from touching the controls — a pilot doesn't want the plane flown for them, they want the *ability to intervene*.

So the real near-term user isn't a chief-of-staff fantasy. It's **a person who periodically inspects an autonomous system and keeps the right to override.** That's less magical and potentially much bigger — and crucially, it's just a **rung on the trust ladder**, not the ceiling. The inspector who spot-checks once a day and finds nothing dropped is already getting the whole value. We don't need users to become outcome-supervisors to win; we need them to climb one rung.

Customer definition (locked, narrow): **people who have already demonstrated willingness to trade privacy for leverage** — the ones who installed Clicky / OpenClaw / Cluely this year. Not "Mac knowledge workers." The narrower it gets, the stronger the company.

---

## 3. The emotional job — relief, not time

People don't buy CRMs, they buy *not dropping deals*. They don't buy insurance, they buy *sleeping at night*. **IDLE's emotional job is relief** — the feeling that nothing important is slipping through the cracks, that your digital life isn't leaking. Not time (people waste time happily). Not convenience (copyable). **Relief.**

This is why the pitch kept drifting from productivity to delegation: relief *is* the product. Implication for brand:

- **"Do Nothing / Always Watching" = the acquisition hook.** Bold, polarizing, gets the install.
- **Relief ("nothing slips through the cracks") = the retention truth.** Why they never uninstall.
- Acquire with the villain; retain with relief. They reconcile: *always watching → nothing slips → you can finally stop worrying.* Don't let the villain voice smother the relief — relief is what we actually measure.

---

## 4. The compounding asset — the execution graph (infrastructure, not memory)

"It learns from you" is a feature investors have heard 8,000 times. The thing that actually compounds is the **execution graph**: after a year IDLE knows who matters, which meetings get accepted vs declined, scheduling patterns, vendors, customers, communication habits, recurring workflows, and the **permission topology** — what it's been trusted to do.

That stops looking like memory and starts looking like **infrastructure**. People rip out apps constantly. They rarely rip out infrastructure. Forking the open-source core gives someone the engine; it does not give them a user's year of configured, permissioned, trusted execution graph. That's the switching cost — and the only candidate moat that doesn't collapse under scrutiny. (Honest: at pre-seed it's a hypothesis to prove, not a moat we have.)

---

## 5. The real product — trust accumulation (a new interface)

Not the agent. **Trust accumulation.** The moment users repeatedly allow software to act without asking, we've created a new interface — and new interfaces are where very large companies come from. The agent is the mechanism; the *accumulation of trust* is the product.

---

## 6. The operating question — the whole company in one sentence

> **What is the minimum set of autonomous actions that causes a user to trust the *next* autonomous action?**

Trust is not binary — it's a ladder, climbed one successful delegation at a time. Understand the sequence and you win. Don't, and you build a thousand capabilities nobody enables. **For the next three months we do not add capabilities. We find the ladder.**

---

## 7. The only three charts that matter

Forget model quality, benchmarks, integrations, frameworks. Obsess over three curves across the first 50 users:

1. **% of actions reverted** → falling toward zero.
2. **% of users with autonomy enabled** → rising.
3. **% of actions requiring approval** → falling.

If all three move in the right direction *simultaneously*, we've discovered **trust accumulation** — the real product. If they don't, we've built an impressive demo for a behavior nobody adopts, and we'll know fast.

---

## 8. The truth test — permissions, not opinions

Don't ask the first 50 whether they like it. **Watch what permissions they grant after 30 days.** People reveal trust with permissions, not words. Day-30 permission depth is the single honest signal of whether this is real.

---

## 9. What changes everywhere else (action items)

- **Reposition the lead:** from "does your morning / inbox" → **"the autonomous execution layer across your apps — you manage outcomes, not software."** (Inbox triage is a graveyard; it's one *input*, never the headline.)
- **North Star metric:** replace "interaction reduction" with **the three charts + day-30 permission depth + the 'very disappointed if it vanished' test.**
- **Roadmap for 90 days:** instrument the trust ladder; map the rung sequence; move the three curves with 50 hand-onboarded users. No new capabilities for their own sake.
- **Brand split:** villain = acquisition; relief = retention. Keep both, assign roles.
- **Moat work:** prove the execution graph compounds into switching cost in the data — or admit there's no moat yet.

---

## 10. The one-paragraph version (lead future conversations with this)

> IDLE is a bet that *operating software* is ending and *managing outcomes* is beginning. The product isn't an assistant — it's **trust accumulation**: the more an always-on agent acts correctly and reversibly on your behalf, the more you let it, until you've stopped opening your apps at all. The emotional job is **relief** — nothing slips through the cracks. The compounding asset is a per-user **execution graph** that becomes infrastructure, not memory. We prove it with three curves — reverts falling, autonomy rising, approvals falling — and with the permissions users grant after 30 days, not what they say. If those move, we haven't built an assistant. We've built a new interface.
